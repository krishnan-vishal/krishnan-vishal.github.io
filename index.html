    THE PAYMENTS NARRATIVE

    Past, Present & Future of Global Payments

    From Bretton Woods to agentic AI settlement — a working narrative of how cross-border payments have evolved, where they stand today, and where the industry is heading next. GPIR's research program documents this history and today's infrastructure while extending forward-looking scenario modelling to 2040 and beyond — new corridors, chapters and data are added on an ongoing basis.

    Past

    Foundations

    1944 – 1990s
    • 1944

      Bretton Woods Agreement

      44 Allied nations anchor the post-war international monetary system to the US dollar, laying the groundwork for modern cross-border finance.

    • 1973

      SWIFT Founded

      239 banks across 15 countries establish SWIFT to standardise financial messaging between institutions worldwide.

    • 1999

      Euro Introduced

      The euro launches as an electronic accounting currency across 11 EU member states, ahead of physical cash entering circulation in 2002.

    Present

    Real-Time & Digital Rails

    2000s – Today
    • 2008

      UK Faster Payments

      One of the world's first nationwide real-time retail payment rails goes live, settling payments in seconds instead of days.

    • 2016

      India's UPI Launches

      The Unified Payments Interface becomes one of the largest real-time payment systems globally, processing billions of transactions every month.

    • 2017

      SWIFT gpi

      Global Payments Innovation introduces end-to-end tracking and same-day use of funds for cross-border payments.

    • 2023

      FedNow & ISO 20022

      The US launches FedNow for instant payments, while major market infrastructures worldwide migrate to the richer ISO 20022 messaging standard.

    Future

    What's Next

    2025 – 2040+
    • 2030s

      Multi-Rail Becomes the Norm

      GPIR research finds no single rail will dominate: SWIFT, domestic real-time networks, stablecoins, tokenised deposits and CBDCs are expected to coexist in an AI-orchestrated, multi-rail infrastructure.

    • 2040E

      Digital Settlement Scales Up

      GPIR's forward model estimates total cross-border flows reaching USD 350–500 trillion by 2040, with stablecoin settlement of USD 100–150 trillion and tokenised deposits of USD 100–300 trillion.

    • 2040E

      AI & Agentic Payments

      GPIR's outlook anticipates 3–4 billion users transacting through AI payment agents by 2040, with wallets surpassing bank apps in daily use and compliance becoming largely automated.

    Source: GPIR, Cross-Border Payments Industry Landscape Report (2021–2025 edition). Figures beyond 2025 are directional industry scenarios, not audited forecasts.

    Why GPIR Exists

    Transforming Published Information into Connected Intelligence

    The global financial ecosystem is evolving faster than ever. Payment innovations, banking transformation, regulatory developments, digital assets, financial infrastructure and emerging technologies continue to reshape the movement of money across the world.

    While trusted information is publicly available, it is often dispersed across regulatory publications, research papers, industry reports and institutional resources.

    GPIR brings these perspectives together through structured curation, editorial interpretation and continuous evolution, creating a single destination for connected financial intelligence.

    About Us

    Global Payments Intelligence Repository (GPIR)

    GPIR is an independent knowledge repository dedicated to documenting and analysing the evolution of global payments, banking infrastructure, financial market infrastructure, fintech ecosystems, digital assets, regulatory frameworks and emerging technologies shaping the future of money movement.

    The repository is designed to support financial institutions, regulators, policymakers, fintech innovators, researchers, academics and industry professionals with structured, evidence-based intelligence from around the world.

    • Independent and research-driven editorial approach
    • Country intelligence covering global payment ecosystems
    • Analysis of payment systems, regulations and infrastructure
    • Executive insights into emerging financial technologies
    • Continuously expanding knowledge repository
    Explore Intelligence →

    Research Principles

    Editorial Independence

    Content is developed using publicly available information, regulatory publications, industry reports and academic research without commercial influence.

    Evidence-Based Research

    Research is supported by recognised sources, market intelligence and documented developments across the global financial ecosystem.

    Global Perspective

    Coverage spans developed and emerging markets, enabling comparative insights into payment innovation and financial infrastructure worldwide.

    Continuous Evolution

    The repository is designed as a living knowledge platform, expanding with new countries, payment systems, regulations and technologies.

    FOUNDER & EDITORIAL IDENTITY

    Founded & Curated by Vishal Krishnan

    A Repository Built for the Long View

    GPIR is built on the belief that understanding payments requires more than following individual transactions, technologies or market developments.

    The repository connects historical evolution, present-day infrastructure, regulatory developments, market intelligence and emerging technologies to create a structured view of how money moves and how financial systems evolve.

    Its purpose is to preserve knowledge, connect evidence and make complex developments easier to understand for professionals, institutions, researchers and decision-makers.

    01

    Editorial Philosophy

    GPIR prioritises evidence, context and connected thinking over promotional narratives.

    02

    Research Principles

    Published information is organised, contextualised and interpreted while maintaining a clear distinction between documented evidence and editorial analysis.

    03

    Repository Governance

    GPIR is designed as a continuously evolving repository, with research, sources, country intelligence and market developments progressively reviewed and updated.

    04

    Long-Term Vision

    To build a connected global reference for understanding payment systems, financial infrastructure and the continuing evolution of money movement.
