Executive Summary

The international education sector has become one of the largest specialised segments within retail cross-border payments. International students generate cross-border payment flows through tuition fees, accommodation, living expenses, insurance premiums, visa processing and examination fees.

The globalisation of education has transformed student mobility into a major payment ecosystem connecting developed education destinations (USA, UK, Australia, Canada, Germany, Singapore, New Zealand) with emerging-market student populations (China, India, Vietnam, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, UAE, South Korea).

3.9–3.10 Regulatory & AML

Education payments generally carry lower AML risk than remittances but remain subject to extensive regulation: student verification, admission verification, beneficiary validation and source-of-funds checks. India's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) governs overseas education and living-expense transfers; China and Nigeria maintain significant FX restrictions affecting student payments.

Key risks include fake universities, fraudulent admissions, money laundering through education channels, visa fraud and document forgery — managed through student identity verification, university verification and payment-purpose validation.

3.16 Chapter Conclusion

Education payments represent one of the most attractive segments in cross-border payments because they combine high transaction values, predictable payment behaviour, strong compliance visibility, low fraud rates and recurring revenue opportunities. By 2030, education payments are expected to approach USD 390 billion annually, making them one of the fastest-growing specialised payment verticals globally.
