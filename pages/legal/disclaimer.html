    1. General Information

    The Global Payments Intelligence Repository (GPIR), available through Fintechoisis, is an independent research, knowledge and intelligence platform focused on the global financial-services ecosystem.

    The Repository documents, organises, contextualises and analyses developments across areas including:

    • Payments;
    • Banking;
    • Cross-border payments;
    • Remittances;
    • Financial infrastructure;
    • Payment networks and payment rails;
    • Fintech;
    • Financial technology;
    • Digital financial services;
    • Financial market infrastructure;
    • Regulatory developments;
    • Digital assets;
    • Emerging technologies; and
    • The broader global money-movement ecosystem.

    All content published through GPIR is provided for general informational, educational, research and analytical purposes only.

    3. Research and Intelligence Purpose

    GPIR is designed to help users research, understand and connect developments across the financial-services ecosystem. The Repository may provide historical research, market intelligence, country intelligence, regional intelligence, regulatory information, industry analysis, data and statistics, charts and visualisations, market comparisons, research publications, forecasts, projections, analytical interpretations, and technology-assisted research tools.

    The purpose of providing such information is to support research and understanding rather than to replace independent professional judgement.

    4. Accuracy and Completeness

    Reasonable efforts are made to research, source, organise and contextualise information before publication. However, GPIR does not guarantee that information available through the platform is complete, accurate, current, error-free, unchanged, suitable for a particular purpose, or continuously available.

    Financial-services information can change rapidly. Regulatory requirements may change, companies may update disclosures, market statistics may be revised, and historical information may subsequently be corrected. Users should verify material information against the relevant primary or authoritative source before relying upon it.

    5. Sources and Third-Party Information

    GPIR may use information originating from central banks, financial regulators, government agencies, international organisations, banks, financial institutions, payment networks, fintech companies, money-transfer organisations, industry associations, academic institutions, research organisations, public databases, company disclosures, and other publicly available sources.

    The inclusion or discussion of information from a third-party source does not constitute endorsement of that source or the organisation to which it relates. Third-party information remains subject to the responsibility and methodology of the original publisher. Where possible, GPIR seeks to identify and reference relevant sources.

    6. Public Information and Editorial Analysis

    GPIR may distinguish between Documented Evidence — information supported by an identifiable source; Derived Data or Analysis — information calculated, organised or interpreted from available evidence; and Editorial Analysis — contextual interpretation, observations or conclusions developed by GPIR.

    Users should consider these distinctions when interpreting Repository content. An analytical statement or interpretation should not automatically be treated as an established fact.

    7. Data, Statistics and Market Intelligence

    The Repository may publish or reference transaction volumes, market sizes, growth rates, market shares, country statistics, corridor data, financial metrics, industry estimates, forecasts, projections, derived calculations, and other quantitative information.

    Data from different sources may use different definitions, reporting periods, currencies, geographic classifications, methodologies, sampling approaches, and measurement techniques. Accordingly, figures that appear comparable may not always be directly comparable. Users are responsible for reviewing source information, methodology and definitions before using data for professional, commercial or other material purposes.

    Certain GPIR content may include forecasts, projections, scenarios, estimates or other forward-looking information. Forward-looking information is inherently uncertain and may be affected by economic conditions, regulatory developments, technological change, market competition, consumer behaviour, currency movements, geopolitical developments, policy decisions, financial-market conditions, and other factors that cannot be reliably predicted.

    Actual outcomes may differ materially from projections. No forecast or projection published by GPIR should be interpreted as a guarantee of future performance or market conditions.

    As GPIR develops, the platform may introduce technology-assisted capabilities including intelligent search, automated research retrieval, country intelligence, regulatory intelligence, data discovery, automated classification, artificial intelligence, machine-learning systems, natural-language processing, research summarisation, knowledge graphs, alerts, and other automated intelligence functions.

    Such tools may retrieve, classify, summarise or interpret information automatically. Automated or AI-assisted results may contain errors, omissions, incorrect classifications, outdated information, incomplete context, or incorrect interpretations.

    Accordingly, intelligence generated through such tools should be treated as a research aid and not as an authoritative source of fact. Users should verify material findings against the underlying source.
