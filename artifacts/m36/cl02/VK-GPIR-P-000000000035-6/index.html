GLOBAL CROSS-BORDER PAYMENTS MARKET (2025E)

USD 208 Trillion

Of this total, wholesale flows (interbank, correspondent banking, and large institutional settlement) account for the substantial majority, while retail cross-border payments — spanning trade, remittances, education, gaming, betting, bill pay, and high-risk digital commerce — form a USD 44 trillion addressable layer with materially higher growth rates and innovation intensity.

Key Takeaways

  • Trade Payments (B2B) remain the dominant retail segment at an estimated USD 42 trillion in 2025, growing at a moderate 5–7% CAGR.

  • Diaspora Remittances (C2C) crossed the USD 1 trillion threshold in 2025, anchored by USA–Mexico and Gulf–South Asia corridors.

  • Emerging digital-native segments — Gaming, Education, Bill Pay, and Stablecoin-enabled B2B — are growing 2–5x faster than legacy trade and remittance flows.

  • High-Risk Payments (USD 980B) and Legalized Betting (USD 82B) represent under-served, higher-margin corridors with concentrated processor activity in Malta, Gibraltar, and the Isle of Man.

  • No single industry database publishes audited cross-border volumes for Gaming, Betting, Bill Pay, or High-Risk segments; figures in this repository are strategic planning estimates synthesized from multiple primary sources (see Section 12: Methodology).

CHAPTER 2: Global Remittance & Diaspora Payments Industry (C2C)

2.0 Introduction

Remittances represent the movement of funds by individuals across international borders for personal, family, educational, healthcare, housing, and small-business support purposes. Unlike trade payments, which are driven by commerce, remittance flows are primarily driven by migration and diaspora communities.

Remittances have become a critical economic pillar for many developing economies, often exceeding foreign direct investment (FDI) and development aid. Countries such as India, Mexico, Philippines, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Egypt, Nigeria, Nepal, and Vietnam rely significantly on inward remittance flows to support household consumption, education, healthcare, housing, and local economic development.

Over the past decade, the remittance industry has evolved from a predominantly cash-based model to a digitally enabled ecosystem driven by account credits, mobile wallets, real-time payment systems, API connectivity, and increasingly, stablecoin-based settlement models.

CHAPTER 10: Emerging Technologies Reshaping Cross-Border Payments (2026–2035)

10.0 Executive Summary

The next decade will witness the convergence of multiple technologies that are individually important but collectively transformational for global cross-border payments. Unlike previous eras of change, where a single technology tended to dominate — SWIFT messaging, card networks, or the early internet payment rails — the future cross-border ecosystem will be built on interoperable layers combining real-time payments, open APIs, digital wallets, artificial intelligence, cloud-native infrastructure, tokenized assets, and digital identity.

European and Asian industry leaders overwhelmingly expect significant transformation in payment infrastructure by 2035, and the technologies profiled in this chapter — Real-Time Payments, APIs, Digital Wallets, Artificial Intelligence, Cloud-Native Infrastructure, ISO 20022, Stablecoins, CBDCs, Digital Identity, and Payment Orchestration — form the connective tissue that links every vertical and every participant analyzed elsewhere in this repository.

10.1 Real-Time Payments Become the Global Standard

Real-time payments (RTP) have moved from being a domestic innovation to becoming the foundation for future cross-border payment infrastructure. Current leaders include UPI (India), PIX (Brazil), PromptPay (Thailand), PayNow (Singapore), NPP (Australia), FedNow (USA), and SEPA Instant (Europe).

Industry participants in both Asia and Europe rank RTP among the most important technologies shaping the future of money movement. Europe's fintech leaders report that 82% see RTP as a core technology for future cross-border payments, while Asia similarly expects RTP interoperability to become central to future infrastructure.

  • Settlement in seconds

  • Reduced liquidity requirements

  • Improved customer experience

  • Lower reconciliation costs

  • Greater financial inclusion

10.2 APIs Become the Universal Payment Language

API-driven connectivity is replacing traditional host-to-host and file-based integrations across the industry. For banks, this means faster partner onboarding, reduced implementation cost, and better data exchange. For fintechs, it enables plug-and-play connectivity, embedded financial services, and rapid market expansion. For corporates, it delivers treasury automation, ERP integration, and real-time cash visibility.

The G20 roadmap specifically identifies API-enabled infrastructure as a critical enabler for meeting future speed, transparency, access, and cost objectives for cross-border payments.

10.3 Digital Wallets Become the New Consumer Bank Account

Digital wallets have become one of the fastest-growing payment instruments globally. By 2026, global digital wallet transaction values are projected to exceed USD 12 trillion, representing substantial growth from prior years.

Major wallet ecosystems include Alipay, WeChat Pay, GrabPay, GCash, LINE Pay, and Paytm across Asia; M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, and Airtel Money across Africa; STC Pay, urpay, and e& money across the Middle East; and Mercado Pago, PicPay, and Nubank across Latin America.

Future cross-border growth will increasingly be wallet-to-wallet rather than bank-to-bank, and wallet interoperability initiatives are already accelerating across Asia.

10.4 Artificial Intelligence and Payment Orchestration

Financial institutions are investing heavily in AI, with industry investment reaching approximately USD 45 billion in 2024. Nearly 70% of firms report AI-related revenue improvements; however, many institutions struggle to realize full value because AI remains disconnected from core business processes. The future lies in AI combined with process orchestration.

  • Compliance — AML monitoring, sanctions screening, fraud detection, KYC automation

  • Treasury — liquidity forecasting, FX optimization, settlement routing

  • Operations — exception handling, automated investigations, customer support

  • Commercial — dynamic pricing, corridor recommendations, customer acquisition

The next generation of payment platforms will become increasingly self-optimizing, using AI-driven decision engines across compliance, treasury, operations, and commercial functions.

10.5 Cloud-Native Infrastructure

Traditional payment infrastructure was designed for batch processing. Future infrastructure requires 24x7x365 availability, global scalability, real-time processing, and instant deployment — capabilities that legacy mainframe-era systems were never architected to deliver.

Cloud-native architectures enable faster innovation cycles, lower infrastructure costs, higher resilience, and easier regulatory adaptation. Cloud adoption is increasingly viewed as mandatory for institutions seeking to achieve G20 cross-border payment targets.

10.6 ISO 20022 and Data Standardization

ISO 20022 is becoming the universal messaging standard across SWIFT, RTP systems, ACH systems, card schemes, and central bank infrastructures. It delivers richer remittance information, structured payment data, and enhanced reconciliation; improved sanctions screening and more accurate AML controls; and better tracking, transparency, and fewer exceptions for end customers.

Industry experts expect ISO 20022 to become one of the most important enablers of payment interoperability over the next decade.

10.7 Stablecoins and Digital Assets

Stablecoins represent one of the most debated innovations in payments. Current leaders include USDT, USDC, PYUSD, and a growing cohort of regulated bank-issued stablecoins.

Future use cases span treasury settlement (weekend funding, intraday liquidity), cross-border payments (emerging market and high-cost corridors), B2B trade (supplier payments, trade settlements), and internal liquidity management (nostro reduction, faster rebalancing).

Industry participants increasingly view stablecoins as a complementary liquidity layer rather than a replacement for traditional payment infrastructure — a theme explored in full depth in Chapter 12.

10.8 CBDCs — Evolution Rather Than Revolution

Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) continue to be explored globally, with examples including the Digital Yuan (China), the Digital Euro (under development), the Digital Pound (research phase), and the mBridge multi-CBDC initiative.

Potential benefits include faster settlement, reduced intermediary costs, and central bank-backed settlement assets. However, private-sector innovation may outpace CBDC implementation timelines, and industry participants remain divided on the ultimate role of CBDCs in cross-border payments.

10.9 Digital Identity and Compliance Automation

One of the largest costs in cross-border payments remains compliance. Future developments include reusable KYC, digital identity wallets, verifiable credentials, and biometric verification — delivering faster onboarding, lower compliance cost, reduced fraud, and an improved customer experience.

This could significantly reduce friction for both retail and business cross-border payments over the coming decade.

10.10 Payment Orchestration Platforms

A major future trend is the rise of orchestration layers sitting above payment rails. Instead of connecting individually to banks, wallets, card schemes, RTP networks, and stablecoin networks, businesses will increasingly connect once to orchestration platforms that intelligently route transactions.

  • Smart routing

  • Dynamic FX

  • Multi-provider redundancy

  • Compliance orchestration

  • Liquidity optimization

This model closely aligns with the future role of platforms such as iSend, Wise Platform, Currencycloud, Banking Circle, Thunes, and NomuPay.

10.11 Strategic Conclusion

By 2035, the winners in cross-border payments are unlikely to be institutions that simply process transactions. They will be firms that successfully combine real-time payment rails, API ecosystems, wallet interoperability, AI-driven orchestration, ISO 20022 data intelligence, cloud-native infrastructure, stablecoin liquidity layers, and digital identity frameworks.

The industry is moving from a world of payment processing to a world of payment orchestration, where value lies not in moving money alone, but in intelligently connecting global financial ecosystems into a seamless, real-time, multi-rail network. This technology convergence underpins every vertical and participant category analyzed throughout the remainder of this repository.

Profile

ASEAN
